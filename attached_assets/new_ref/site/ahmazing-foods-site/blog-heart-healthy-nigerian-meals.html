What Makes a Meal "Heart-Healthy"? A Nigerian Kitchen Guide

AHmazing Foods · Healthy Meals

"Heart-healthy" gets used loosely enough that it stops meaning anything. In practical, kitchen terms — for someone managing blood pressure — it usually comes down to three things: sodium, fibre, and the type of fat you're cooking with.

Sodium is the biggest lever

Seasoning cubes are the main hidden source of sodium in most Nigerian cooking — far more than the salt shaker itself. Building flavour from fresh pepper, onion, garlic, ginger, and herbs, and using seasoning cubes sparingly (or not at all), is the single change that moves the needle most.

Fibre slows everything down

Fibre-rich vegetables and beans don't just help blood sugar — they're also associated with better blood pressure outcomes, partly because meals built around them tend to naturally displace some of the sodium-heavy, processed components of a plate.

Not all fat is equal

This isn't about eating zero fat — it's about which fat, and how much. A soup finished with a controlled amount of oil, using more vegetable-forward ingredients to carry flavour, sits differently than one where oil is the primary flavour vehicle. Grilling or boiling protein instead of deep-frying it is the other half of this.

Portion size still matters

A heart-healthy ingredient list doesn't mean an unlimited portion. Swallow and rice portions in particular are worth being deliberate about — not eliminated, just sized to match the rest of the plate rather than dominating it.

What this looks like on a real plate

  • A modest portion of swallow or rice, not a mountain of it
  • A vegetable-forward soup or side taking up real space on the plate
  • Grilled or boiled protein rather than fried
  • Herbs, pepper, and aromatics doing the seasoning work instead of seasoning cubes

Our Healthy Meals menu is built around exactly this approach. It's home cooking with these principles applied consistently — not a prescription, and not a replacement for guidance from your doctor or a registered dietitian, especially if you're already on medication for blood pressure.
